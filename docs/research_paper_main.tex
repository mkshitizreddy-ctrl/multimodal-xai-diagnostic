\begin{abstract}
This work evaluates DenseNet-121 for binary pneumonia classification from
pediatric chest X-rays, with Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)
attention and post-hoc Grad-CAM explanations. On a 624-image held-out test
split, the vision model achieved an AUROC of 0.9604 (95\% CI: 0.9412--0.9757)
and an AUPR of 0.9628 (95\% CI: 0.9377--0.9825). A late-fusion configuration
using synthetic tabular attributes improves point estimates further; this
demonstrates that the implementation can exploit correlated multimodal
signal, not clinical validity with real patient records. Extending the CBAM
comparison from three to five random seeds revealed that its apparent
benefit on both ranking (AUROC) and Grad-CAM lung-localization was not
statistically distinguishable from noise ($p=0.67$ and $p=0.57$
respectively). By contrast, an attention-consistency loss that explicitly
penalizes CBAM's spatial attention for falling outside the segmented lung
field produced a trade-off that strengthened, not weakened, under the same
five-seed extension: a statistically significant localization improvement
($p=0.038$) at a statistically significant AUROC cost ($p=0.012$). We
separately show that both the vision and fusion models are miscalibrated
(Expected Calibration Error $\approx 0.135$, concentrated in the
highest-confidence bin), that two standard post-hoc corrections
(temperature scaling, isotonic regression) fail to meaningfully improve
this, and that training with label smoothing does, without a statistically
confirmed cost to ranking performance on either model. We report all
thresholded and ranking metrics with bootstrap confidence intervals and
identify and correct a training non-determinism issue discovered during
this replication work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Pneumonia remains an important use case for computer-aided interpretation of
chest radiographs. Deep neural networks can achieve high classification
performance, but visual explanations must be interpreted cautiously: a
plausible heatmap does not establish clinically valid reasoning. This work
therefore combines classification evaluation with Grad-CAM-based localization
measurement.

\section{Related Work}
CBAM applies sequential channel and spatial attention with limited additional
overhead~\cite{woo2018cbam}. Recent pneumonia studies have combined CBAM,
DenseNet-121, and Grad-CAM, although their task definitions and data splits
differ from this binary study~\cite{dey2026cbam,potharaju2025enhanced,
rashid2025framework}. Grad-CAM is widely used for weakly supervised
localization~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam,shahi2025weakly}. DenseNet variants
have also been evaluated directly on the Kermany chest X-ray data set
\cite{bundea2024pneumonia,erukude2025explainable}. Temperature scaling is a
standard post-hoc confidence-calibration method for modern neural
networks~\cite{guo2017calibration}; label smoothing, originally proposed as
a regularizer, has separately been shown to improve model
calibration~\cite{muller2019labelsmoothing}.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Architecture}
An ImageNet-pretrained DenseNet-121 backbone produces a 1024-dimensional image
representation. CBAM is inserted after feature extraction and before the
classification head. For the fusion configuration, four tabular inputs are
encoded into a 64-dimensional representation and concatenated with the image
representation before classification.

\subsection{Explainability}
Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} produces class-specific heatmaps from the
final convolutional layer. A pretrained lung-segmentation model provides lung
masks. The lung-energy fraction is the fraction of heatmap activation contained
inside the segmented lung region. This is a quantitative proxy for anatomical
localization, not a clinical validation of the explanation.

\subsection{Attention-Consistency Training}
Rather than only measuring lung-energy fraction after training, an
attention-consistency loss term, $1 - \text{(lung-energy fraction)}$, is
added to the training objective with weight $\lambda$, explicitly penalizing
CBAM's spatial attention map for placing activation outside the segmented
lung field. Resulting checkpoints are architecturally identical to a
standard CBAM-enabled vision model; only the training objective differs.

\subsection{Calibration}
Expected Calibration Error (ECE) is computed with ten equal-width confidence
bins, alongside Brier score, on the held-out test set. Three calibration
correction strategies are evaluated: temperature
scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration}, isotonic regression, and training-time
label smoothing~\cite{muller2019labelsmoothing}. The first two are post-hoc,
fit on validation data and applied to test-set logits or probabilities; the
third retrains the model with softened training targets.

\section{Dataset and Preprocessing}
We use the pediatric Chest X-Ray Pneumonia data set~\cite{kermany2018identifying},
containing 5,856 images (1,583 normal and 4,273 pneumonia). The original
624-image test split is retained. The training and validation pools are
re-split using the best available patient identifiers; this reduces leakage
risk but cannot prove complete patient-level separation for normal images,
which lack equivalent identifiers. Images are resized to $224\times224$,
replicated from grayscale to RGB, and normalized. Training images receive
horizontal-flip and small-rotation augmentation.

\section{Limitations}
The data set is pediatric and from a single public source; performance may
not generalize across hospitals, imaging devices, age groups, or disease
prevalences. The fusion metadata are synthetic and deliberately
label-correlated, so they must not be interpreted as real clinical evidence.
Grad-CAM and the lung-energy fraction are explanatory proxies rather than
ground-truth lesion localization measures. The CBAM and attention-consistency
vision-only comparisons were extended to five seeds; the weight-sensitivity
sweep and the fusion-model CBAM comparison remain at three seeds and their
associated $p$-values are still exploratory. The calibration correction
attempts, particularly isotonic regression, were constrained by a modest
validation set (798 images); its degraded performance on the vision model is
consistent with overfitting rather than a genuine limitation of the method
itself. Label smoothing was evaluated at a single value ($\epsilon=0.1$) and
a single seed per model; a subsequent smoothing-value sweep at one seed
showed calibration improving non-monotonically across values, and multiple
seeds per value would be needed to identify an optimal value with
confidence. A training non-determinism issue -- insufficiently seeded
data-augmentation randomness and non-deterministic GPU convolution
algorithms -- was identified during this work and corrected; results
reported before the fix (the original three-seed comparisons and the
original attention-consistency estimate) may carry additional unquantified
variance from this source.

\section{Conclusion}
DenseNet-121 provides strong held-out ranking performance for this research
prototype, and the fusion configuration improves the reported point
estimates further, though its synthetic metadata prevent clinical
conclusions. CBAM attention alone did not produce a statistically reliable
effect on either ranking performance or lung localization once evaluated
across five seeds, despite an encouraging three-seed result. An explicit
attention-consistency loss, by contrast, produced a genuine and strengthening
trade-off under the same extended replication: significantly improved lung
localization at a significant, quantified cost to AUROC. Separately, both
the vision and fusion models are shown to be meaningfully overconfident;
among three correction strategies evaluated, only training-time label
smoothing improved calibration without a statistically confirmed cost to
ranking, on both models. Future work should validate on external,
patient-linked data, compare explanations with expert annotations, and
extend the remaining three-seed comparisons and the label-smoothing value
sweep to five seeds for full statistical consistency with the results
reported here.
